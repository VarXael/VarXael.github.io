Project Sasha

"The world does not exist until you scan it into memory."

An android in a dark megastructure, where everything she knows costs memory. I wrote two design documents for the same rule. These are both of them built as playable slices, so the difference between them can be felt, not just read.

What changes

v1 · Design Docv2 · Master Doc
LookingGhost scan: reserves the object's full weight in volatile memory.Soft scan: free, and it fades a few seconds after you look away.
CommittingFull scan, one object at a time, written to the area chip.One flash charge saves everything still lit within range, at once.
The voidA probability: walk on an unscanned bridge and you fall.Solid: you can only walk on floor you have saved. There is no falling.
What it costsMemory, all the time.Flash charges. Containers gamble them: a locker gives cells back, a bin costs 5 MB for nothing.
ChipsSwapped at a terminal; one rides inside an enemy.Carried in Sasha's spine. They overlap the same space and can be merged.
EnemiesTouch a warden and you die.Wardens scan you back, and are immune until you spend a flash to render them.

What the two builds show. v1 makes every glance expensive, so the player scans less and reads more. v2 makes looking free and puts the price on the decision to keep, which turns each flash into a bet on what is lit. Same law, a different economy, and a very different pace.
